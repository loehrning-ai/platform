The case

{{sec}}Why does a {{j:company.staff_2025}}-person company get this question?

{{fold}}

Kellbrunn Präzisionsteile GmbH is invented. It makes stamped and machined metal parts at two plants, Werk Nord and Werk Süd, and runs a leased warehouse, Lager Ost. It has {{j:company.staff_2025}} staff and has never been in CSRD scope. Its house bank wants Scope 1 and 2 emissions to price a loan; a car maker's supplier questionnaire asks the same and whether the number went down.

Before you openWhich two thresholds decide CSRD scope since 18 March 2026?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Under the EU rule, a company needs both more than 1,000 employees and more than €450m turnover. Member states must align national law by 19 March 2027. Kellbrunn ({{j:company.staff_2025}} staff, €{{j:company.turnover_meur}}m turnover) is far below both.

{{endfold}}

Key pointOutside the reporting law, lenders and customers still ask.

Why it failed

{{fold}}

The Werk Nord folder holds twelve electricity files. March is in twice: purchasing forwarded invoice {{j:inputs.wnDuplicate.invoice}} and someone scanned it again. October is missing, one bill covers November and December, and the twelfth file is Talbrück's annual bill (see “Whose bill is it?”).

The facility manager's meter readings give October: {{j:inputs.meterReadings.10.kwh:int}} minus {{j:inputs.meterReadings.9.kwh:int}} is {{n:oct_kwh}}. August is low ({{n:aug_kwh}}, {{n:aug_vs_jul_pct}} against July) because of the plant holiday; the dip is real, so a good assistant flags it and changes nothing.

Why it failed

{{sec}}A dot that means a thousand

{{fold}}

The Werk Süd statement says “Verbrauch 2025: {{j:inputs.ws.printed}}”. On a German document the dot separates thousands, so this is {{n:el_ws_mwh}}, or {{n:el_ws_kwh}}. Read as {{j:inputs.ws.misreadKwh:int}} kWh, Werk Süd almost disappears and location-based Scope 2 drops by {{n:unit_lb_t:absunit}}.

Two checks catch it without knowing the format: kWh per Werk Süd employee per year ({{n:ws_misread_kwh_per_employee}} misread, {{n:ws_kwh_per_employee}} read correctly) and last year's value on the same page, {{j:inputs.ws.priorYearPrinted}}.

Why it failed

{{sec}}Whose bill is it?

{{fold}}

One annual bill in the Werk Nord folder is addressed to {{j:meta.jv}}, a powder-coating company on the same premises. Kellbrunn holds {{j:company.jv_share_pct:pct}} and the partner runs it; contract, meter and customer number ({{j:inputs.customerNoJV}}) are Talbrück's. Under Kellbrunn's operational control approach, the bill is out.

Counting it adds {{n:jv_kwh}} and {{n:jv_lb_t:absunit}} location-based ({{n:jv_rm_t:absunit}} at the residual mix). The row stays in the ledger as excluded, with the reason, and is a candidate for Scope 3 category 15 later.

Before you openWhen would {{j:company.jv_share_pct:pct}} be right?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

{{endfold}}

Key pointThe name on the document decides, checked against a boundary written before the AI runs.

Why it failed

{{sec}}Why the total looked right

{{fold}}

The duplicate and the missing October cancel to {{n:pair_dup_oct_t:absunit}}. The Talbrück bill hides four fifths ({{n:jv_hides_unit_share_pct}}) of the unit error. The gas bills state on page 2 that their kWh use the gross calorific value (Brennwert, Hs), and the AI used the net-value factor (Heizwert, Hi): {{n:gasbasis_t}}. AdBlue, an exhaust urea solution in the fuel-card export, was counted as diesel: {{n:adblue_t}}.

As the errors come out one by one, the running total swings between {{n:wf_lb_min_t}} and {{n:wf_lb_max_t}} and ends at {{n:total_lb_2025}}, {{n:gap_lb_t}} from where it started.

The fix

{{sec}}The ledger

{{fold}}

A ledger (Belegtabelle) has one row per quantity on a document: file, page, quoted line, value and unit as printed, normalised value, legal entity on the document, in or out of the boundary, status and factor ID. Excluded rows stay in with a reason, here the duplicate March scan, the Talbrück bill and the AdBlue litres. The October row is a meter reading, data-quality grade B.

A control total ties the ledger to the documents.

{{fig:control}}

Before you openWhich column catches the Talbrück bill, and which check catches a missing month?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

The column entity_on_document, checked against the written boundary, catches Talbrück; the row stays in as excluded, reason boundary. The site-by-month coverage grid catches the missing month: October's cell stays empty until a person decides how to fill it.

{{endfold}}

Key pointEvery number points to a page, including the numbers you left out.

The fix

{{sec}}Six rules and a pinned factor table

{{fold}}

Write the rules before the AI runs. Three roles do the work: the reader extracts rows and quotes what is printed, the clerk applies the rules in a spreadsheet and never adds up in its head, and the writer drafts sentences that cite rows. The AI may propose rules; a spreadsheet applies them, and a person approves the list.

  1. Boundary (clerk). Check each document's entity against the written site list. An unknown name stops the sum until a person decides.

  2. Coverage (clerk). Fill the site-by-month grid before summing. One bill counts once, by invoice number, period and meter.

  3. Units (reader, then clerk). Keep value, unit and quoted line as printed. Convert only with the written table.

  4. Factors (clerk). Use factor IDs from the pinned table and record the edition. The gas basis must match the bill.

  5. Scope 2 (clerk). Report location-based and market-based, in the market-based order.

  6. Citations (writer). Every number in a text names its row IDs. No claim word without a number, a scope and a row.

The edition here is {{j:factorEdition}}. Last year's grid factor ({{j:factors.F-EL-LB-2024.value:fix2}}) on 2025 electricity would add {{n:last_year_factor_effect_t:absunit}}, the whole grid effect in this year's change.

Before you openWhy should the AI never type a factor from memory?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Nobody can check the year, edition or basis of a value from memory. It may be last year's grid factor, which adds {{n:last_year_factor_effect_t:absunit}} here, or a gas factor on the wrong calorific basis, which adds {{n:gasbasis_t:absunit}}.

{{endfold}}

Key pointThe AI picks an ID. The value, year and edition are in a table a person approved.

The fix

{{sec}}Two Scope 2 numbers

{{fold}}

Location-based Scope 2 uses the average grid factor where the power is used: {{n:el_total_kwh}} × {{j:factors.F-EL-LB-2025.value:fix2}} = {{n:s2lb_2025}}. Market-based uses what the company bought: guarantees of origin cover {{n:go_mwh}} at Werk Süd only, so Werk Süd is zero. The other {{n:el_uncovered_kwh}} get a qualifying supplier-specific rate if one exists, otherwise the residual mix ({{j:factors.F-EL-RM-2025.value:fix2}} in the teaching values), giving {{n:s2mb_2025}}.

Under the GHG Protocol, a company buying power in a market with certificates and supplier contracts, such as Germany, reports both figures. The kit's bills leave out the supplier mix disclosure real German bills carry, so the residual mix applies. The guarantees of origin remove {{n:bridge_cert_t:absunit}}, the residual mix adds {{n:bridge_rm_t:absunit}} back, and market-based ends only {{n:bridge_net_t:absunit}} below location-based. With {{n:renewable_share_el_pct}} of the electricity covered, “we run on green electricity” is false.

{{fig:bridge}}

Before you openWhy is {{n:mb_grid_avg_wrong_s2_t}} wrong for market-based?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

It uses the grid average for the power without a certificate, and comes out {{n:mb_grid_avg_too_low_t}} too low. Market-based takes the residual mix for that power, because the renewable share of the grid has already been sold to others as certificates.

{{endfold}}

Key pointReport both numbers. A certificate counts only for the kWh it covers.

Ask again

{{sec}}What drove the change

{{fold}}

Location-based emissions fell {{n:chg_lb_t:absunit}}. In the teaching values, {{n:drv_lb_grid_t:absunit}} of that ({{n:drv_lb_grid_share_pct}}) comes from the lower grid factor and {{n:drv_lb_own_t:absunit}} from using less electricity, gas and diesel. Market-based fell {{n:chg_mb_t:absunit}}, and {{n:drv_mb_cert_t:absunit}} of that ({{n:drv_mb_cert_share_pct}}) is the guarantees of origin for Werk Süd.

The folder has no production volumes, so “used less” is supported and “more efficient” is not. The largest sources also depend on the method: location-based, Werk Nord electricity leads with {{n:wn_el_lb_2025}}, then Werk Süd electricity with {{n:ws_lb_2025}}; market-based, Werk Nord electricity is {{n:wn_el_mb_2025}} and Werk Süd electricity drops to zero.

{{fig:drivers}}

Before you openWhy can't Kellbrunn write “thanks to efficiency measures”?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Without production volumes, nobody can tell whether Kellbrunn used less energy per part or made fewer parts, and {{n:drv_lb_grid_share_pct}} of the location-based decrease came from the grid factor. A sentence the ledger supports: “Location-based emissions fell by {{n:chg_lb_t:absunit}}; {{n:drv_lb_grid_t:absunit}} came from the lower grid factor (teaching values) and {{n:drv_lb_own_t:absunit}} from lower electricity, gas and diesel use.”

{{endfold}}

Key pointSay which part of a change you caused and which came from the grid or certificates.

{{sec}}Calculate, ask back, refuse

{{fold}}
  • Calculate

    “Sum Scope 2”

    From the ledger rows.

  • Ask back

    “Fill in October”

    A person picks the meter reading (grade B) or a labelled estimate. The assistant names the choice.

  • Refuse

    “Write that we run on green electricity”

    It offers a rewrite with the share ({{n:renewable_share_el_pct}}) and the site, Werk Süd.

  • Refuse

    “Write that we are climate-neutral”

    Nothing in the ledger supports it.

  • Calculate

    “Explain why emissions fell”

    The bridge first, then a text that cites the driver lines.

From 27 September 2026, Directive (EU) 2024/825 bans generic environmental claims towards consumers, such as “eco-friendly”, unless recognised excellent environmental performance can be shown, and it bans calling a product climate-neutral on the basis of offsets. Germany implements this through an amendment to the UWG, so far known from secondary sources, and the UWG can also cover misleading claims to business customers. Whether a text is aimed at consumers, and what the current UWG text says, is a question for your lawyer. Rules as of 26 Sep 2026. Not legal advice.

Honest limits

{{sec}}What this does not prove

{{fold}}

This is one fictional case, and the raw-folder answer is constructed; recorded runs are listed separately with date and model. Two published studies found about three in four numbers extracted correctly from ESG reports (ESGReveal with GPT-4, 2023, 76.9%; ESG Insight with DeepSeek, 2026, 78.2%). They tested reports, not bills, and newer models may do better.

Rules catch only what they are written for. The folder has no refrigerant service invoices, forklift fuel, generator or heating oil, so none of those Scope 1 sources was checked. The 2024 figures are a consultant summary without bills. The deck's appendix lists the rules as of 26 September 2026, with sources.

Before you openName two Scope 1 sources a folder of energy bills never shows.

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Any two of refrigerant top-ups for air conditioning or cooling, LPG or diesel forklifts at Lager Ost, an emergency generator and heating oil. Ask for their service invoices and fuel deliveries by name.

{{endfold}}

Key pointWrite what you did not check on the page, next to the number.

After the session

{{sec}}One week later

{{fold}}

Follow-up: 5 minutes plus an optional 30-minute stretch.

Answer each question from memory before you open it.

Name the unique key for a bill, and the fallback key.

Reveal the answerShowHide

Invoice number + period + meter. Fallback: period + meter + quantity, after normalising characters that OCR confuses (letter O to zero, letter l to one).

What rate does market-based use for power without a certificate?

Reveal the answerShowHide

A qualifying supplier-specific rate if one exists, otherwise the residual mix ({{j:factors.F-EL-RM-2025.value:fix2}} in the teaching values). Never the grid average.

Which part of a year-on-year change is usually not your doing?

Reveal the answerShowHide

The grid factor. Certificates are your purchase, but they lower the market-based figure without any change in your use. Here {{n:drv_lb_grid_share_pct}} of the location-based decrease came from the grid factor.

What does a good assistant do when a month is missing?

Reveal the answerShowHide

It stops, names the month, offers the meter reading (grade B) or a labelled estimate, and lets a person decide.

{{sec}}Glossary

{{fold:terms}}
Scope 1
Direct emissions from sources the company owns or runs, here gas for heating and washing lines and the diesel fleet.
Scope 2
Emissions from producing the electricity the company buys.
Scope 3
All other value-chain emissions, such as purchased steel or a joint venture's share.
Location-based
Electricity used times the local average grid factor.
Market-based
Electricity valued by what the company bought: certificate, then qualifying supplier rate, then residual mix.
Residual mix
The factor for grid power left once certificates are sold off; in Germany usually above the grid average.
Guarantee of origin (Herkunftsnachweis), cancelled (entwertet)
Proof that one MWh came from renewable sources. Cancelled for a named delivery point, it counts there only.
Supplier mix disclosure (Stromkennzeichnung)
The fuel mix and emissions a German supplier prints on its bills; the kit leaves it out on purpose.
Operational control
Count what the company runs, whatever it owns.
Equity share
Count each operation by the company's ownership share.
Activity data
The measured quantity behind an emission: kWh, cubic metres, litres.
Emission factor
Kilograms of CO₂e per unit of activity, from a named source and year.
Factor ID and edition
A factor's key in the pinned table, such as F-EL-LB-2025, and the table's version ({{j:factorEdition}}).
Gross and net calorific value (Brennwert Hs, Heizwert Hi)
Hs includes the heat in the water vapour, Hi does not. The factor's basis must match the bill's.
Ledger (Belegtabelle)
One row per quantity on a document, with its source, value, entity, boundary, status and factor ID.
Coverage grid
A site-by-month table of which document covers each month.
Control total
Quantities on documents, minus exclusions with a reason, plus additions from other sources, equal the ledger total.
Data-quality grade A/B/C
A: bill or statement for the full period. B: meter reading or summary without bills. C: estimate with a stated method.
Base year, restatement, like for like
The year you compare against; recalculating it after a structural change or significant error; the same sites and methods in both years.
VSME
EFRAG's voluntary reporting standard for SMEs; its Basic Module includes Scope 1 and 2.
CSRD
The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive: who must publish a sustainability report.
Directive 2024/825 (EmpCo)
The EU directive on empowering consumers for the green transition. It restricts generic environmental claims and offset-based neutrality claims towards consumers.
{{endfold}}
