Before the route

{{sec}}Why does a {{j:company.staff_2025}}-person company get this question?

Kellbrunn Präzisionsteile GmbH is invented. It makes stamped and machined metal parts at two plants, Werk Nord and Werk Süd, and runs a leased warehouse, Lager Ost. It has {{j:company.staff_2025}} staff and has never been in CSRD scope. Its house bank asks for Scope 1 and 2 emissions to price a loan, and a car maker's supplier questionnaire asks the same, plus whether the number went down. The question for the whole workshop is: “{{j:question.en}}”

Before you openWhich two thresholds decide CSRD scope since 18 March 2026?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

A company must have more than 1,000 employees and more than €450m turnover, both. That is the EU rule; member states must bring national law in line by 19 March 2027. Kellbrunn, with {{j:company.staff_2025}} staff and €{{j:company.turnover_meur}}m turnover, is far below both.

Key pointA company outside the reporting law still gets asked, by the people who lend it money and buy its parts.

Before you openWhich of the five checks would you have trusted most, and what does it actually test?

Reveal the explanationShowHide
  • A normal-looking change tests the total against last year's total. Errors that cancel pass it.
  • Tonnes per employee tests the same total, divided by headcount.
  • Twelve bills counts files. One March bill is in twice, one bill covers November and December, and one belongs to another company.

Why it failed

{{sec}}Twelve files, eleven months

The Werk Nord folder holds twelve electricity files. March is in twice: purchasing forwarded the invoice and someone scanned it again, so a second file with invoice number {{j:inputs.wnDuplicate.invoice}} is next to the first. October is missing. The November and December bill covers two months, which keeps the count at twelve.

The facility manager's meter readings show October: {{j:inputs.meterReadings.10.kwh:int}} minus {{j:inputs.meterReadings.9.kwh:int}} is {{n:oct_kwh}}. The duplicate adds {{n:dup_kwh}} and the gap removes {{n:oct_kwh:bare}}, so the Werk Nord sum is only {{n:wn_net_raw_vs_right_kwh:absunit}} off. August is low ({{n:aug_kwh}}, {{n:aug_vs_jul_pct}} against July) because of the plant holiday. That is real, and a good assistant flags it for a person without changing it.

Why it failed

{{sec}}A dot that means a thousand

The Werk Süd statement says “Verbrauch 2025: {{j:inputs.ws.printed}}”. On a German document the dot separates thousands, so this is {{n:el_ws_mwh}}, or {{n:el_ws_kwh}}. Read as {{j:inputs.ws.misreadKwh:int}} kWh, Werk Süd almost disappears and location-based Scope 2 drops by {{n:unit_lb_t:absunit}}.

Two checks catch it without knowing the number format: {{j:inputs.ws.misreadKwh:int}} kWh would be {{n:ws_misread_kwh_per_employee}} per Werk Süd employee per year, against {{n:ws_kwh_per_employee}} when read correctly, and the same page prints last year's value, {{j:inputs.ws.priorYearPrinted}}.

Why it failed

{{sec}}Whose bill is it?

One annual bill in the Werk Nord folder is addressed to {{j:meta.jv}}, a powder-coating company on the same premises. Kellbrunn holds {{j:company.jv_share_pct:pct}} and the partner runs it; the contract and the meter are Talbrück's, and the bill carries Talbrück's own customer number, {{j:inputs.customerNoJV}}. Counting it adds {{n:jv_kwh}} and {{n:jv_lb_t:absunit}} location-based ({{n:jv_rm_t:absunit}} at the residual mix). The row stays in the ledger as excluded, with the reason, and is a candidate for Scope 3 category 15 later.

Why it failed

{{sec}}Why the total looked right

Six errors are inside the first answer. The duplicate and the missing October cancel to {{n:pair_dup_oct_t:absunit}}. The Talbrück bill hides four fifths ({{n:jv_hides_unit_share_pct}}) of the unit error. The gas bills state on page 2 that the kWh are based on the gross calorific value (Brennwert, Hs), and the AI used the factor for the net value (Heizwert, Hi): {{n:gasbasis_t}}. AdBlue in the fuel-card export is a urea solution for the exhaust, not a fuel: {{n:adblue_t}}.

When you remove the errors one by one, the running total swings between {{n:wf_lb_min_t}} and {{n:wf_lb_max_t}} and ends at {{n:total_lb_2025}}, {{n:gap_lb_t}} away from where it started.

The fix

{{sec}}The ledger

A ledger (Belegtabelle) has one row for each quantity on a document. Each row keeps the file, the page, the quoted line, the value and unit as printed, the normalised value, the legal entity on the document, whether it is inside the boundary, the status and the factor ID. Excluded rows stay in with a reason: the duplicate March scan, the Talbrück bill and the AdBlue litres. The October row is a meter reading, so it gets data-quality grade B.

A control total ties it together: all electricity on documents ({{n:ctl_docs_kwh:bare}}) minus what was excluded with a reason ({{n:ctl_excluded_kwh:bare}}) plus October from the meter ({{n:ctl_meter_kwh:bare}}) equals the {{n:ctl_total_kwh}} in the ledger.

{{fig:control}}

Before you openWhich column catches the Talbrück bill, and which check catches a missing month?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

The column entity_on_document, checked against the written boundary, catches the Talbrück bill. Its row stays in with the status excluded and the reason boundary. The site-by-month coverage grid catches the missing month: October has no bill, so its cell stays empty until a person decides how to fill it.

Key pointEvery number points to a page, including the numbers you left out.

The fix

{{sec}}Six rules and a pinned factor table

The rules are written before the AI runs: boundary, coverage, units, factors, Scope 2 and citations. The work splits into three roles. The reader extracts rows and quotes what is printed. The clerk applies the rules in a spreadsheet, never by mental arithmetic. The writer drafts sentences that cite rows. The AI may propose rules; a spreadsheet applies them, and a person approves the list.

  1. Boundary (clerk). Check the entity on each document against the written list of sites. A name not on the list stops the sum until a person decides.

  2. Coverage (clerk). Fill the site-by-month grid before summing. One bill counts once, by invoice number, period and meter.

  3. Units (reader, then clerk). Keep value and unit as printed, with the quoted line. Convert only with the written table.

  4. Factors (clerk). Use factor IDs from the pinned table and record the edition. The gas basis must match the bill.

  5. Scope 2 (clerk). Report location-based and market-based, in the market-based order.

  6. Citations (writer). Every number in a text names its row IDs. No claim word without a number, a scope and a row.

Factors come from a pinned table by ID, with the edition recorded ({{j:factorEdition}}). Using last year's grid factor ({{j:factors.F-EL-LB-2024.value:fix2}}) on 2025 electricity would add {{n:last_year_factor_effect_t:absunit}}, which is the whole grid effect in this year's change.

Before you openWhy should the AI never type a factor from memory?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

A value from memory has no year, no edition and no basis that anyone can check. It may be last year's grid factor, which here adds {{n:last_year_factor_effect_t:absunit}}, or a gas factor on the wrong calorific basis, which adds {{n:gasbasis_t:absunit}}. A factor ID points to one row in a table a person approved.

Key pointThe AI picks an ID. The value, year and edition are in a table a person approved.

The fix

{{sec}}Two Scope 2 numbers

Location-based Scope 2 uses the average grid factor where the power is used: {{n:el_total_kwh}} × {{j:factors.F-EL-LB-2025.value:fix2}} = {{n:s2lb_2025}}. Market-based Scope 2 uses what the company bought. The guarantees of origin cover {{n:go_mwh}} at Werk Süd and nothing else, so Werk Süd is zero. The other {{n:el_uncovered_kwh}} get, in order, a qualifying supplier-specific rate if one exists, otherwise the residual mix: {{j:factors.F-EL-RM-2025.value:fix2}} in the teaching values, giving {{n:s2mb_2025}}.

Under the GHG Protocol a company buying power in a market with certificates and supplier contracts, such as Germany, reports both figures. The kit's bills leave out the supplier mix disclosure that real German bills carry, so here the residual mix applies. The certificate removes {{n:bridge_cert_t:absunit}} and the residual mix adds {{n:bridge_rm_t:absunit}} back, so market-based is only {{n:bridge_net_t:absunit}} below location-based. “We run on green electricity” is false; {{n:renewable_share_el_pct}} of the electricity is covered.

{{fig:bridge}}

Before you openWhy is {{n:mb_grid_avg_wrong_s2_t}} wrong for market-based?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

It uses the grid average for the power without a certificate, and comes out {{n:mb_grid_avg_too_low_t}} too low. Market-based takes the residual mix for that power, because the renewable share of the grid has already been sold to others as certificates.

Key pointReport both numbers, and count a certificate only for the kWh it covers.

{{sec}}What drove the change

Location-based emissions fell {{n:chg_lb_t:absunit}}. In this case's teaching values, {{n:drv_lb_grid_t:absunit}} of that ({{n:drv_lb_grid_share_pct}}) comes from the lower grid factor and {{n:drv_lb_own_t:absunit}} from using less electricity, gas and diesel. The folder has no production volumes, so “used less” is supported and “more efficient” is not. The largest sources also depend on the method: location-based, Werk Nord electricity leads with {{n:wn_el_lb_2025}}, then Werk Süd electricity with {{n:ws_lb_2025}}; market-based, Werk Nord electricity is {{n:wn_el_mb_2025}} and Werk Süd electricity drops to zero.

Before you openWhy can't Kellbrunn write “thanks to efficiency measures”?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Without production volumes nobody can tell whether Kellbrunn used less energy per part or made fewer parts. And {{n:drv_lb_grid_share_pct}} of the location-based decrease came from the grid factor, which Kellbrunn did not cause. Honest limits

{{sec}}Calculate, ask back, refuse

Five requests show the three jobs of a useful assistant.

  • Calculate“Sum Scope 2”

    A calculation from the ledger.

  • Ask back“Fill in October”

    Needs a decision: the meter reading (grade B) or a labelled estimate.

  • Rewrite“Write that we run on green electricity”

    Gets rewritten with the share ({{n:renewable_share_el_pct}}) and the site (Werk Süd).

  • Refuse“Write that we are climate-neutral”

    Gets refused, because nothing in the ledger supports it.

  • Explain“Explain why emissions fell”

    Gets the bridge first, then a draft that cites driver lines.

For context: since 27 September 2026, Directive (EU) 2024/825 bans, towards consumers, generic environmental claims such as “eco-friendly” unless recognised excellent environmental performance can be shown, and claims that a product is climate-neutral based on offsets. In Germany this happens through an amendment to the UWG, known from secondary sources; check the current text. Whether a text such as your website is aimed at consumers is a question for your lawyer. Misleading claims are banned towards business customers too (in Germany § 5 UWG). Rules as of 26 Sep 2026. Not legal advice.

Before you openWhich request needs a person to decide, and what is the decision?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

“Fill in October.” A person chooses between the meter reading, which gets grade B, and a labelled estimate, and the ledger records who chose. The assistant names the choice and does not make it silently.

Key pointA good assistant asks back when a person has to decide, and names the decision.

Honest limits

{{sec}}What this does not prove

This is one fictional case. The raw-folder answer is constructed; recorded runs, with date and model, are listed separately. Two published studies measured about three in four numbers extracted correctly from ESG reports (ESGReveal, GPT-4, 2023: 76.9%; ESG Insight, DeepSeek, 2026: 78.2%). They tested reports, not bills, and newer models may do better.

Rules catch only what they are written for: the folder has no refrigerant service invoices, no forklift fuel, no generator and no heating oil, so none of those Scope 1 sources was checked. The 2024 figures are a consultant summary without bills. Factors are teaching values. Rules are as of 26 September 2026 (the deck's appendix lists what is true, with sources). This is not legal or audit advice.

Before you openName two Scope 1 sources a folder of energy bills never shows.

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Any two of these: refrigerant top-ups for air conditioning or cooling, LPG or diesel forklifts at Lager Ost, an emergency generator, heating oil. Ask for the service invoices and fuel deliveries by name, and write next to the number which of them you did not check.

Key pointWrite what you did not check on the page, next to the number.

{{sec}}Glossary

Scope 1
Direct emissions from sources the company owns or runs, here the gas for heating and washing lines and the diesel fleet.
Scope 2
Emissions from producing the electricity the company buys and uses.
Scope 3
All other emissions in the value chain, such as purchased steel or a joint venture's share.
Location-based
Scope 2 method: electricity used times the average grid factor where it is used.
Market-based
Scope 2 method: electricity used, valued by what the company bought, in the order certificate, qualifying supplier-specific rate, residual mix.
Residual mix
The factor for electricity that is left once certificates have been sold off the grid; in Germany usually higher than the grid average.
Guarantee of origin (Herkunftsnachweis), cancelled (entwertet)
A certificate that one MWh came from renewable sources; cancelled for a named delivery point, it counts for that point and nowhere else.
Supplier mix disclosure (Stromkennzeichnung)
The fuel mix and emissions a German supplier prints on its bills; the kit's bills leave it out on purpose.
Operational control
Boundary approach: count all of what the company runs, and none of what it does not run, whatever it owns.
Equity share
Boundary approach: count each operation by the company's ownership share.
Activity data
The measured quantity behind an emission, such as kWh, cubic metres or litres.
Emission factor
Kilograms of CO₂e per unit of activity, from a named source and year.
Factor ID and edition
The key of a factor in the pinned table, such as F-EL-LB-2025, and the version of the table ({{j:factorEdition}}).
Gross and net calorific value (Brennwert Hs, Heizwert Hi)
Hs includes the heat in the water vapour, Hi does not; the factor's basis must match the basis printed on the bill.
Ledger (Belegtabelle)
One row per quantity on a document, with file, page, quote, value as printed, normalised value, entity, boundary, status and factor ID.
Coverage grid
A site-by-month table that shows which document covers each month, filled before anything is added up.
Control total
All quantities on documents, minus what was excluded with a reason, plus additions from other sources, must equal the ledger total.
Data-quality grade A/B/C
A: bill or statement for the full period. B: meter reading or summary without bills. C: estimate with a stated method.
Base year, restatement, like for like
The year you compare against; recalculating it after a structural change or a significant error; comparing the same sites and methods in both years.
VSME
EFRAG's voluntary sustainability reporting standard for small and medium-sized companies; its Basic Module includes Scope 1 and 2.
CSRD
The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, which sets who must publish a sustainability report.
Directive 2024/825 (EmpCo)
The EU directive on empowering consumers for the green transition; it restricts generic environmental claims and offset-based neutrality claims towards consumers.
