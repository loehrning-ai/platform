# Solutions

## 1. Baseline

a) Not scored: 03.02.2025 (no forecast stored; the week drops out for every method,
   otherwise you compare different weeks) and 10.03.2025 (sold out from Thursday:
   the actual is smaller than the demand). 04.11.2024 is sold out too, but lies
   before the check window. 16.09.2024 appears twice with the same values: count it
   once. That leaves 50 weeks.
b) Forecast: mean error 33.3 jars, bias +3.0 %.
   "Same as last week": 42.2 jars, bias −0.3 %.
c) "Same as last week": 92.6 %. The gap of 1.6 percentage points means 21 % smaller
   errors (1 − 33.3 ÷ 42.2). The tool was closer in 30 of 50 weeks: a single week
   proves nothing. For comparison: "same week last year" 38.8, "average of 4 weeks"
   42.2. Against the better rule "same week last year" it is 14 %.
d) Promotion at the organic market, 20 % off. Actual 637, forecast 671, "same as last
   week" 470. The tool knows the promotion calendar.

## 2. Range

b) 36 of 50, 40 were promised. Before 07.04.: 9 of 12. From 07.04.: 27 of 38.
   11 of the 14 misses fall from 07.04. on.
c) An organic market chain with 9 stores delists the soup, about 70 jars a week
   fewer. The tool keeps planning at the old level; the errors turn one-sided and the
   actual falls below the range. Chance alone gives 36 or fewer in about 11 of 100
   years; that the misses cluster after one date points to the cause.

## 3. Planning number

a) 1.50 ÷ 1.90 = 0.79.
b) 748. Range of the week 694 to 756, median 731. In 5 of the last 26 weeks 748
   would not have been enough, in 19 something would have been left over.
   Cost of the errors in 2025: "same as last week" €2,052, forecast €1,105,
   forecast plus buffer €1,023.
c) A leftover jar costs €1.60: share 1.50 ÷ 3.10 = 0.48, planning number 729. With
   nearly equal costs the forecast itself is the planning number. Costs 2025: €3,270,
   €2,621, €2,497.

## 4. Rule

a) 37 weeks free, 15 stopped. 03.02.2025: data (no forecast). 21.04. to 21.07.2025:
   bias above 5 % and fewer than 9 of 13 inside the range; on 28.04. "same as last
   week" was also better (ratio 1.05). Highest bias: +10.6 % on 16.06.2025. Free
   again from 28.07.
b) Two weeks: delisting 07.04., first red week 21.04.
c) The person named in the release rule, with a number and a reason: forecast minus
   about 70 jars, reason delisting. In the study by Fildes et al. (2009), large
   adjustments with a known reason tended to improve accuracy, small ones from gut
   feeling tended to make it worse.
