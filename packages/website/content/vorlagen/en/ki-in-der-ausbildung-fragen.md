# AI in apprenticeships: questions for youth representatives and works councils

Use this list when your company wants to bring an AI tool into apprenticeship training, for example an AI tutor, a learning platform with AI features or a writing assistant for reports. The youth and trainee representation (Jugend- und Auszubildendenvertretung, JAV) collects the questions, and the works council puts them to the employer. Each question names its legal basis and the points a useful answer contains. The legal references are German statutes, namely BetrVG (Works Constitution Act), BBiG (Vocational Training Act) and JArbSchG (Youth Employment Protection Act), and EU law, namely the GDPR and the EU AI Act.

**Last editorial review:** 27 September 2026. This is not legal advice. The list explains the law in general terms and does not assess any individual case. Licence CC BY 4.0, attribution: loehrning.ai, Tim Löhr, https://loehrning.ai/en/blog/ki-in-der-ausbildung

## How to use this list

1. The JAV collects what trainees want to know about the tool and discusses it (Section 70(1) no. 3 BetrVG).
2. The JAV asks the works council to put the topic on the next agenda (Section 67(3), Section 70(1) no. 1 BetrVG). If the topic particularly concerns trainees, the whole JAV may attend that item (Section 67(1) BetrVG).
3. The works council puts the questions to the employer and asks for the documents it needs (Section 80(2), Section 90(1) no. 3 BetrVG).
4. The works council passes the answers and documents on to the JAV (Section 70(2) BetrVG) and brings the JAV into meetings with the employer when trainees are particularly affected (Section 68 BetrVG).
5. If the works council has to assess the AI, bringing in an expert counts as necessary. The works council agrees the details with the employer (Section 80(3) BetrVG). This right belongs to the works council; the JAV does not have it.

The JAV has no co-determination rights of its own towards the employer. It acts through the works council.

## A. Plan and tool

### 1. Which AI tool is planned for training, for what purpose, and from when?

Legal basis: Section 90(1) no. 3 and (2), Section 80(2) BetrVG

The answer should contain: product, provider and version, the purpose in one sentence, the occupations and training years affected, the start date, and whether it is a pilot or permanent. The employer provides the documents, such as the product description, the privacy notice and the contract with the provider.

Note: The employer informs the works council at the planning stage and consults early enough for its proposals to be taken into account. Section 90 BetrVG gives no veto.

### 2. How does the company classify the tool under the AI Act?

Legal basis: Article 6 and Annex III points 3 and 4 AI Act; Section 80(3) BetrVG

The answer should contain: whether it is an AI system, the company's role (usually deployer), and whether the tool evaluates learning outcomes (including to steer the learning path), monitors exams or prepares personnel decisions. Annex III lists these uses as high-risk cases, and their obligations apply from 2 December 2027. Article 6(3) AI Act allows exceptions. If the provider relies on one, its documented assessment (Article 6(4) AI Act) belongs in the documents.

Open: Whether a learning platform in a training company falls under Annex III point 3 is not settled. The Commission's guidelines on classification were only a draft when this list was reviewed.

## B. Data

### 3. What data about trainees does the tool record?

Legal basis: Section 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG; Article 5(1)(c) and Article 13 GDPR

The answer should contain: a list of data types with their purpose, such as inputs, learning time, progress, test results, usage logs and device or location data. For each type it says whether the data is linked to a person and who needs it.

### 4. Where is the data processed, how long is it kept, and does the provider use it to train its own models?

Legal basis: Article 5(1)(e), Article 28 and Articles 44 et seq. GDPR

The answer should contain: the provider and server location, the data processing agreement, retention periods for each data type and a clear statement on whether inputs are used to train models. If data goes to countries outside the EU, the answer names the legal basis for that transfer.

### 5. What may trainees enter into the tool, and what must never go in?

Legal basis: Section 13 sentence 2 no. 6 BBiG; Article 9 GDPR

The answer should contain: a written rule with examples of allowed inputs, a list of data that never goes in (customer data, trade and business secrets, health data, data about colleagues), and who trainees can ask when they are unsure.

## C. Evaluation and monitoring

### 6. Can the tool evaluate the performance or behaviour of individual trainees?

Legal basis: Section 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG; Article 35 GDPR

The answer should contain: which functions make this technically possible (overviews, rankings, exports, logs) and which of them are switched off or restricted. If people are assessed, the answer includes the result of the data protection impact assessment.

Note: Under the case law of the Federal Labour Court (Bundesarbeitsgericht), it is enough that a system the employer introduces or provides is objectively suited to record or evaluate data on performance or behaviour (for example BAG, order of 13 December 2016, 1 ABR 7/15). The company's intention does not matter for this. In that case the works council has a right of co-determination.

### 7. Who sees which results?

Legal basis: Section 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG; Article 5(1)(b) GDPR

The answer should contain: an overview of roles (trainee, trainer, line manager, HR, provider) and what each role sees. It also states whether individual results or only aggregated figures are visible, from what group size, and who may export data.

### 8. Does anything from the tool feed into appraisals, the probation period or the decision to keep a trainee on?

Legal basis: Section 94(2), Section 70(1) no. 1 BetrVG; Article 22 GDPR

The answer should contain: a clear yes or no for each of these purposes. If the answer is yes, it names the data, the criteria and the person who makes the final decision. A decision with legal or similarly significant effect may be based solely on automated processing only in the exceptional cases of Article 22(2) GDPR.

Note: AI used to evaluate performance and behaviour is one of the high-risk cases in Annex III point 4 AI Act, and their obligations apply from 2 December 2027. General appraisal principles already need the works council's consent today.

### 9. Does the tool detect or evaluate feelings or moods, for example through the camera, the voice or typing patterns?

Legal basis: Article 5(1)(f) AI Act

The answer should contain: a clear no, backed by the provider's documentation. Similar functions, such as an attention or motivation score, are named and checked.

Note: Emotion recognition in the workplace and in education institutions has been prohibited since 2 February 2025, except for medical or safety reasons. According to the Commission's guidelines on prohibited practices, this covers emotions inferred from biometric data such as the face or the voice.

Open: Whether an attention score falls under the prohibition depends on what it measures and what it infers it from.

## D. Learning, training record and exams

### 10. Does the tool steer or assess learning?

Legal basis: Section 98(1) BetrVG; Section 14(1) no. 1 BBiG; Annex III point 3(b) AI Act

The answer should contain: whether the tool only makes suggestions or sets tasks, order and learning goals, who checks its assessments and can overrule them, and how this fits the company's training plan.

Note: The works council co-determines how in-company vocational training measures are carried out, including apprenticeship training.

### 11. Which tasks does the tool take over that trainees used to learn from?

Legal basis: Section 14(1) no. 1 BBiG; Section 96(1), Section 97(2) BetrVG

The answer should contain: which activities change and how trainees still learn the skills behind them, for example first without AI and then by checking and correcting AI output. It also names the resulting change to the training plan.

Note: If an employer measure changes the work so that existing knowledge and skills are no longer enough, the works council co-determines the introduction of in-company training measures.

### 12. What applies when trainees write their training record (Berichtsheft) with AI?

Legal basis: Section 13 sentence 2 nos. 6 and 7, Section 14(2) BBiG

The answer should contain: a written rule on whether this is allowed and with which tool. An entry describes what really happened and contains no trade secrets. The trainer reviews the record regularly, and trainees get the opportunity to keep it at the workplace.

Note: The law does not say what the record is written with. Whether it is kept in writing or electronically is set in the training contract (Section 11(1) BBiG). For admission to the final exam it has to be submitted in writing or electronically through the training company (Section 43(1) no. 2 BBiG).

### 13. What applies to AI in exams and project work?

Legal basis: Section 47 BBiG and the exam regulations of the competent body

The answer should contain: the link to the current rules of the responsible chamber, meaning which aids are allowed, how to disclose AI use and what counts as cheating. It also says who informs trainees about this before the exam.

Note: A company rule does not change the exam regulations. The rules on AI differ from chamber to chamber.

## E. Trainees under 18

### 14. Does the tool set young trainees a pace, daily targets or rankings?

Legal basis: Section 23(1) no. 3, Section 22(1) no. 1, Section 8(1) JArbSchG

The answer should contain: whether there are time limits, countdowns, daily targets or comparisons with others, and how they are switched off for trainees under 18. It also says in which hours the tool is used. Young people may work at most 40 hours a week and, as a rule, at most eight hours a day. Section 8(2) and (2a) JArbSchG allow eight and a half hours on some days.

Open: Young people may not do work where their pace is set or forced more than occasionally. How this applies to a learning tool is a question of interpretation.

### 15. Have the risk assessment and the safety instruction for young people been updated?

Legal basis: Section 28a, Section 29 JArbSchG

The answer should contain: whether the company treats the introduction as a significant change in working conditions, what the risk assessment says about psychological strain and screen time, and when the instruction on the tool takes place.

Note: Instruction for young people is repeated at least every six months.

## F. Training, time and costs

### 16. What training do trainees and training staff receive, when, and in which hours?

Legal basis: Article 4 AI Act; Section 96(1), Section 97(2), Section 98(1) BetrVG

The answer should contain: content, duration and date, whether the training takes place in paid working or training time, whether it covers all occupations and what is documented. The content covers the limits of the tool, checking results and the rules on data.

Note: Article 4 AI Act has applied since 2 February 2025. As amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force since 27 July 2026, it requires providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures that support the development of AI literacy of their staff. Article 4 does not require a specific level, a fixed training format or a formal attestation.

Open: Whether training under Article 4 AI Act is subject to co-determination under Section 98 BetrVG is not settled. No decision of the Federal Labour Court on this was known when this list was reviewed.

### 17. Who provides accounts and devices, and do trainees bear any costs?

Legal basis: Section 14(1) no. 3 BBiG

The answer should contain: who provides accounts, licences and devices, whether trainees are expected to use private accounts and whether any costs fall on them.

Open: The company provides training materials free of charge. Whether an AI licence counts as one is not settled and depends on whether it is necessary for the training.

## G. Choice, problems and review

### 18. What is mandatory, what is voluntary, and what happens if someone opts out?

Legal basis: Section 75(2) BetrVG; Article 7(3) and (4) GDPR

The answer should contain: which use is part of the training and which is voluntary, another way to learn or document for the voluntary parts, and a commitment that saying no has no disadvantages. If anything relies on consent, the answer explains how to withdraw it.

Note: The European Data Protection Board considers consent in employment to be freely given only in exceptional cases, because of the dependency (Guidelines 05/2020 on consent). With minors there is the added question of whether they can judge the consequences.

### 19. Who do trainees turn to when something goes wrong with the tool?

Legal basis: Sections 84, 85 and 69 BetrVG

The answer should contain: a contact person for the tool, the route for complaints and error reports, and how issues reach the JAV and the works council. Nobody may be put at a disadvantage for making a complaint.

Note: In establishments that normally employ more than 50 young employees and trainees, the JAV can hold consultation hours during working time.

### 20. When will the company check whether the tool helps learning, and what happens when new functions arrive?

Legal basis: Section 90(2), Section 96(1), Section 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG

The answer should contain: a date for the first review, the criteria (learning results, workload, errors, feedback from trainees), who takes part and what happens after a poor result. It also contains a commitment to inform the works council before new functions are switched on, so that it can involve the JAV.

Note: If an update adds a new evaluation function, this can trigger co-determination under Section 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG again.

## What this list does not cover

The list applies to establishments with a works council under the Works Constitution Act. In the public sector the Federal Staff Representation Act (BPersVG) or the laws of the federal states apply, and churches have their own rules.

If AI helps select applicants for apprenticeships, you need separate questions. Selection guidelines need the works council's consent even when AI helps draw them up (Section 95(2a) BetrVG), and the high-risk obligations for AI in recruitment under Annex III point 4 AI Act apply from 2 December 2027.

The list does not replace advice on an individual case. Help is available from the works council, the trade union for its members, the training advisers of the responsible chamber (Section 76 BBiG) and employment lawyers. Laws and chamber rules change, so check the current legal text before any negotiation.
