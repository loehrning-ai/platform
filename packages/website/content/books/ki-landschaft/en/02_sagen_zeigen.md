# Saying and showing

The text pattern found the word AI on {{c3.regex_hits}} of the {{c4.own.n}} company websites with their own address. I wanted to know what stands behind the word.

For that, the source code of each of these pages was searched for four traces: a chat widget or AI tool, a hint of machine learning or a case study, a posted AI job and rules for handling AI, such as a reference to the European AI regulation. A trace counts only if it appears on the page itself.

Then {{c3.read_sample}} homepages were read. Of the pages that mention AI, {{c3.read_narrow.num}} percent show something concrete under the narrow reading (chatbot, machine learning technique, AI job, AI rules), with an interval of {{c3.read_narrow.range}}, and {{c3.read_broad.num}} percent under the broad reading (any named AI function and any AI offer), with {{c3.read_broad.range}}. The number depends on what you call concrete.

## Which traces exist

Most often the pattern found a chat widget or AI tool (whether AI sits behind it, the page does not say), on {{c3-evidence.tool.num}} percent of the pages with a hit. Next comes the hint of machine learning or a case study at {{c3-evidence.ml.num}} percent. AI jobs and AI rules were so rare that I give no share for them. A page can show several traces, so the shares do not add up.

This has limits. The traces are text patterns in the source code, and a chatbot with canned answers counts just like a learning system. Held against the pages that were read, {{c3.trace_precision.num}} percent of the hits showed concrete use (interval {{c3.trace_precision.range}}), and the patterns found {{c3.trace_recall.num}} percent of the pages that show it ({{c3.trace_recall.range}}, broad reading). An AI assistant read the pages, and my cross-check is still open.

The number says nothing about what the firm actually does, how well it works and whether anyone uses it at all, because only what stands on the page was read, and that is little.

## What does not follow

It does not follow that firms without visible proof do nothing with AI. It means only that the text pattern found nothing on their page with which a visitor could check the word. Either the firm uses something and keeps quiet about it, or it writes AI because everyone does.

These data cannot separate which of the two explanations is more common, and a word on a website is therefore a weak signal, in both directions.

Whoever reads your page, customer or applicant alike, can only check what is documented there. A sentence with AI and no proof remains a claim.
