# The iceberg problem

The visible debate about AI often focuses on software development and new models. A large share of the work where AI could matter in practice is found in administration, finance, customer service, procurement, and other processes that are rarely described as "AI work."

## What the Iceberg Index measures

Chopra et al. published the research paper [The Iceberg Index: Measuring Workforce Exposure Across the AI Economy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25137) in 2025. The model covers 151 million workers in the United States, more than 32,000 skills, and thousands of AI tools.

The authors report two main measures:

| Measure | Result in the model | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Visible exposure | 2.2% of US wage value | concentrated in computer and technology occupations |
| Broader technical exposure | 11.7% of US wage value | additional cognitive tasks in administration, finance, and professional services |

These figures measure a modelled overlap between tasks and technical capabilities. They do not measure actual adoption, productivity gains, job losses, or a share of the German market.

## What an organization can infer from this

The US findings must not be presented as German statistics. The underlying question is still useful: Which tasks involve recurring information work, and under what conditions could a system support them?

Start with tasks, not products:

- gather information from several sources
- classify or structure content
- prepare drafts
- flag discrepancies
- document decisions
- summarize recurring status information

Technical feasibility is only one factor. Data quality, the consequences of errors, human oversight, data protection, security, worker participation, and domain accountability determine whether a use is appropriate and lawful.

## The two inventories

A sound inventory separates two questions:

1. **Which AI systems and features are already in use?** Record approved and unapproved accounts, embedded features, integrations, and automated decisions.
2. **Which tasks could be supported?** Record volume, the cost of errors, data classes, required domain review, and measurable success criteria.

The first inventory establishes control. The second produces a prioritized list for learning and pilots. Neither requires a company ranking or an externally calculated readiness score.

## The right starting point

Start with a process whose input data is approved, whose output a person reviews before use, and whose success can be measured. Document the baseline, test representative cases, and record errors.

The iceberg problem is not a failure to spot a spectacular AI application. It is a failure either to examine everyday information work systematically or to improve it under controlled conditions.
