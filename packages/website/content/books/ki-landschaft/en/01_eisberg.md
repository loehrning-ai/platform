# The number that does not add up

How many companies in Germany use AI? Ask around in autumn 2026 and you get answers between 20 and 57 percent, and they come from surveys that state their questions and bases, with the exception of ifo, which names neither.

The gap does not come from mistakes. Each survey asks different firms a different question. [KfW](https://www.kfw.de/PDF/Download-Center/Konzernthemen/Research/PDF-Dokumente-Fokus-Volkswirtschaft/Fokus-2026/Fokus-Nr.-554-Juli-2026-KI_Mittelstand.pdf) asked mid-sized firms whether they had used any AI application in the years 2022 to 2024 and arrived at 20 percent. That counts firms with fewer than five employees and anyone who used AI at any point in those three years. The [Federal Statistical Office](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Branchen-Unternehmen/Unternehmen/IKT-in-Unternehmen-IKT-Branche/IKT-U-Erhebung/info.html) reports 26 percent for 2025, but it looks only at firms with at least ten employees in selected sectors, leaving out agriculture, the financial sector and many service firms.

::figure{id="C1"}

The [ifo Institute](https://www.ifo.de/fakten/2026-06-05/mehr-als-die-haelfte-der-unternehmen-nutzt-kuenstliche-intelligenz) gives 54.5 percent for May 2026, but its results page names neither the sample nor the exact question, while at [Bitkom](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Erstmals-nutzt-die-Mehrheit-der-Unternehmen-KI) it is 57 percent among firms with at least 20 employees, surveyed from July to August 2026, with 603 responses. A trend over the years cannot simply be read off either. The Federal Statistical Office changed its counting unit in 2025, so its jump from 20 to 26 percent is not a clean trend. Compare only what was measured the same way.

A thought experiment shows how much room there is. A business with eight employees that now and then tries a text tool in the office counts at KfW, because it includes the smallest firms and a long time window. The Federal Statistical Office never asks it, because it starts at ten employees, and Bitkom misses it too, because only firms with at least 20 employees answer there.

A range is therefore more honest than a single number. How many firms use AI depends on who asks and which firms they invite, and on how they explain AI before they put the question.

## The iceberg is a US model

The second number that is often passed on wrongly comes from the USA. Chopra and colleagues published the [Iceberg Index](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25137) in October 2025. The model represents 151 million workers and asks how much wage value hangs on tasks that AI could technically perform, whether or not a business uses it for them today. The model puts visible AI use at 2.2 percent of wage value, in technical occupations. Technically possible would be 11.7 percent, because administration, finance and professional services then count as well.

::figure{id="C2"}

Both values are model calculations for the American labour market. They measure how far tasks and technical abilities overlap, but neither use in businesses nor layoffs and certainly not the German market. Whoever cites them as a German statistic puts words in the source's mouth that appear nowhere in it.

The iceberg still works as a question to you: which tasks in your firm consist of recurring information work, and who checks today whether a system may help with them? No study supplies the answer, it lies in your processes.

> **What you can do:** For every AI number, first ask measured how and among whom. Write the question and the base next to the number before you take it into a presentation. If one of the two is missing, cite the number as an example and not as a share.
