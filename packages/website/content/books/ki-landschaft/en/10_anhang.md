# Appendix: terms, sources, and limitations

## Glossary

| Term                   | Definition                                                                                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **AI Act**             | Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 with obligations based on role, system, and risk.                                                                   |
| **Provider**           | Actor under Art. 3 No. 3 AI Act; the classification follows the actual conduct and can change under Art. 25.                                  |
| **Deployer**           | Actor under Art. 3 No. 4 AI Act that uses an AI system under its own authority, except in a personal non-professional activity.               |
| **Data flow**          | Documented movement of data among people, devices, services, regions, subprocessors, logs, and backups.                                       |
| **DPIA**               | Data protection impact assessment under Art. 35 GDPR when processing is likely to result in a high risk.                                      |
| **FRIA**               | Fundamental rights impact assessment under Art. 27 AI Act for the addressees and high-risk deployment specified there.                        |
| **High-risk AI**       | AI systems that fall under Art. 6 with Annex I or III; exceptions and the specific intended purpose have to be checked.                       |
| **AI literacy**        | Skills, knowledge, and understanding under Art. 3 No. 56; Art. 4 requires context-appropriate measures, not a specific certificate.           |
| **Human oversight**    | The organizational and technical ability of competent, authorized people to understand, monitor, override, or stop a system.                  |
| **Pilot**              | Limited, measurable test with defined data, users, controls, stop criteria, and evaluation.                                                   |
| **Technical exposure** | Overlap between tasks and technical system capabilities; not the same thing as adoption, automation, or job displacement.                     |

- [Chopra et al. (2025): The Iceberg Index](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25137). A US model of technical task exposure, not of German adoption rates or displacement.
- [Bitkom (11 March 2026): digitalization of the economy](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Digitalisierung-der-Wirtschaft-Unternehmen-beschaeftigen-sich-mit-KI). Representative survey of 604 companies in Germany with at least 20 employees; the results do not carry over automatically to microenterprises below that threshold.

## Claims removed or not used

This book uses no private company database, no proprietary scoring engine, no regional or sector company rankings, no competitive analysis. Percentages, samples, signal weights, and readiness values derived from such sources are not in the open learning materials.

Market forecasts and product rankings from commercial secondary sources are missing too, as long as no reliable primary source and no clear method stand behind them.

## Limitations

1. US labor-market exposure is not a German forecast of adoption or employment.
2. Company surveys measure reported use and judgement, not technical effectiveness.
3. Public web features do not reliably show internal processes, data quality, and controls.
4. Product features, prices, contracts, data flows, and the legal position change. Volatile information needs a review date and a primary source.
5. This book replaces no legal, data protection, security, or professional review of the specific use.
