# EU AI Act: assess roles and obligations precisely

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 has been in force since 1 August 2024 and becomes applicable in stages. This chapter was reviewed on 9 August 2026 and takes account of the regulation that entered into force on 27 July 2026, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. For an actual decision, always use the [current text on EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32024R1689) and the [official EU timetable](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai).

## Establish the system, purpose, and role first

A product name does not answer a legal question. Document:

- which function is actually active
- the intended purpose stated by the provider
- how your organization uses it
- whether you are a provider, deployer, or another operator
- which people, data, and subsequent decisions are affected

Obligations differ by role. A deployer does not automatically assume the provider's conformity assessment. A substantial modification, use under your own name, or use for a changed purpose can trigger a change of role under Art. 25.

| Date            | Selected points of application                                                                      |

| 2 February 2025 | Chapter I, Art. 4, and the prohibitions in Art. 5                                                   |

| 2 December 2027 | current official EU timetable for high-risk systems under Annex III                                 |
| 2 August 2028   | current official EU timetable for high-risk systems embedded in regulated products under Annex I    |

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026. Among other matters, it amends Article 4, the dates for high-risk systems, and individual transitional rules.

## Art. 4: AI literacy depends on context

Providers and deployers must take measures to support the development of AI literacy among their staff and other people dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf. Technical knowledge, experience, education and training, use context, and the people or groups affected must be taken into account. A specific individual level of competence does not have to be guaranteed.

The [European Commission Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers) clarifies:

- no uniform mandatory training for every role
- no prescribed certificate
- no prescribed "AI officer"
- no general minimum number of hours

Brief general training may be insufficient for a risky use case. Conversely, not everyone needs the same depth of legal and technical training. Document the role, need, measure, and effectiveness review.

## Do not conflate prohibitions, high risk, and transparency

### Art. 5

Art. 5 prohibits precisely defined practices, including certain manipulative or exploitative systems, certain forms of social scoring, certain biometric categorization, emotion recognition in workplaces and educational institutions in principle, and other cases described there. Use the statutory text and its exceptions, not a shortened list of labels.

### Art. 6 with Annexes I and III

High-risk status follows from the statutory classification and intended purpose. Annex III includes certain systems used in employment, education, essential services, law enforcement, migration, and the administration of justice, among other areas. Art. 6(3) contains an exception for Annex III systems that requires close assessment; profiling of natural persons continues to receive particular treatment.

Provider and deployer obligations differ. Deployers of high-risk systems should examine Art. 26 in particular: instructions for use, oversight, controlled input data, monitoring, responses to risks or incidents, logs, information for employees, and use-case-specific information for affected people.

### Art. 50

Art. 50 does not impose a general obligation to label every AI-generated text. It contains distinct obligations:

- Providers of certain interactive systems inform people that they are interacting with AI, unless this is obvious.
- Providers of generative systems ensure machine-readable marking of certain outputs.
- Deployers of emotion-recognition or biometric-categorization systems inform affected people under the conditions set by law.
- Deployers of deepfakes and certain texts on matters of public interest have separate disclosure obligations and exceptions.

## Other applicable law

The GDPR, trade-secret protection, copyright, labor and co-determination law, equal-treatment law, and sector-specific rules do not apply only after an AI Act high-risk classification. An AI Act classification does not replace review under these regimes.

## Starter checklist

1. Inventory the system, version, function, purpose, users, and affected people.
2. Substantiate the role of each operator.
3. Assess Art. 5, Art. 6 with its annexes, and Art. 50 separately.
4. Check the application date of the specific obligation.
5. Assess the data flow, contracts, security, and other applicable law.
6. Define Art. 4 measures suited to the roles and risks.
7. Document approval, oversight, logs, escalation, incidents, and review.

The free course at `/ki-fuehrerschein` can be one learning measure. Completing it is not a certificate required by law and does not, by itself, prove that Art. 4 has been met for a specific use case.
