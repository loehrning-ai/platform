The material — where the numbers come from

Nobody reads 2,115 rows in a meeting — that's why reports exist.

Every figure in the report is a roll-up of rows like these. The report says CRAFT did €4.12M — the receipts agree, to the euro: 95 September rows, 9,162 units, €4,124,238. A good analyst reads the story and checks the receipts. In prompt 3, yours will do exactly that — live.

The first analyst move — what to measure

You can't track everything — pick the few numbers that decide the month.

A monthly report holds hundreds of figures. Your job is choosing the few that answer: was this a good month, and what do we do next? For NORDA, five — plus one flag.

Revenuethe month's size, in list-price contribution
Unitsvolume shipped, separate from price
Defects (NCRs)logged non-conformances — production defects, by line
Marketing spendcampaign cost by channel
Open escalationsunresolved exec-level complaints
The flagthe line to interrogate before you scale it

the small E# chips on the page are the report's own evidence references — every figure names its source

The analyst work — definitions

Same word, different meaning per company — and the definition drives the call.

Revenue
Reads like a “high-margin premium.” Here it's units × list price — list-price contribution, not gross margin: the data carries no per-SKU cost.
Defects (NCRs)
Sounds like “returns.” Here it's logged production defects per line — a quality signal, not customer returns.
Marketing
Looks like “spend behind CRAFT.” Here it's campaign spend by channel — it doesn't split cleanly per line.

Read CRAFT's €450 as “high margin” and you'd scale it — but it's list price, not margin. Defining the number is the skilled work you take to your own company.

Read together — the month's question

Read together, the numbers point at one line — CRAFT.

CRAFT is the #2 revenue line — €4.12M — on the #6 volume, yet it carries the heaviest defect load of any line, two months running: 23 NCRs in September after 24 in August. (A dash in the NCR column means no records logged that month, not zero defects.)

The habit that transfers — any report, any company

Prove every number, then argue the other side.

Two prompts carry to any monthly report, whatever the company: trace every figure to its source, then stress-test the call against itself.

Trust prompt 01 — sourcing · we run this one live nowFor every number in your last answer, show me exactly which file and column it came from. Which did you infer rather than read?
Trust prompt 02 — adversarial · take-home, any reportArgue the opposite recommendation as convincingly as you can. What would have to be true for it to win?
