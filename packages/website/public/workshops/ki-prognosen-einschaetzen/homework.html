Your three tiers

DOPlay the hands-on lab

15 min

Open the hands-on lab. In Act 1 (parcel network) switch the forecast between History only, + Weekday and + Promo dates. In Act 2 (device maker) move the Service target slider and compare Local buffers with Shared forecast. The gap between forecast and order is your safety stock.

  1. Hit a high service level. Run Act 2 at a service target of ≥ 90%. Feel what it takes to almost never stock out, and watch the cash tied up in inventory grow.
  2. Beat the manual rule once. In Act 1, find the forecast that beats “same weekday last week” on the capacity bill, and note the one shock that no forecast catches.
  3. Write down the buffer that worked. Record the service target that balanced cost and fill rate, then reason about which business deserves the bigger buffer: a bakery (croissants spoil overnight) or a parcel network (a missed delivery costs more than an idle van). The critical ratio decides, and the answer may surprise you.
Business lesson: chasing 100% service level loses money. The best buffer is set by the cost of over- vs under-forecasting: Pillars 1, 2, 3 in one decision.
Trains: over · under · buffer. Field card §2 (newsvendor) & §3 (safety stock)

STRETCHYour first honest forecast

45 min

Make two honest forecasts on a practice dataset and score them. No coding required; a spreadsheet is enough. Coders can use any notebook.

Dataset  demand-weekly.csv: 104 weeks of a product's demand (week_start, units).
Two years, weekly, with trend + a Q4 bump. Hide the last 14 rows as your test set, or bring your own series.
  1. Split. Hide the last ~14 rows (your test period). Never let the model see the answers first. This is backtesting.
  2. Baseline: naive. Forecast each test day as yesterday's actual (or last week's same day if there's a weekly rhythm). This is the bar to beat.
  3. Smoothed: pick one. Use a spreadsheet moving average (mean of last 3), or exponential smoothing F = 0.3·actual + 0.7·F_prev; notebook: SimpleExpSmoothing/Holt.
  4. Score both over 14 days: MAE = AVG(ABS(actual - forecast)); Bias = AVG(forecast - actual) (positive = over-forecast, negative = under).
  5. Report four numbers (MAE + Bias for naive and smoothed) and one sentence: did smoothing earn its keep?
Trains: baselines + honest scoring. Field card §5 (reading a forecast) and the mindset box

CHALLENGEYour own domain

open

You know a business, team, or system where something runs out or piles up. Apply the S&OP / newsvendor lens in exactly 5 sentences:

  1. Name your domain and the one thing whose demand you'd forecast (staff hours? inventory? server capacity? ER beds? ad budget?).
  2. Where does over-forecasting cost you most?
  3. Where does under-forecasting cost you most?
  4. Which is worse, and is your critical ratio closer to a high or low service level?
  5. What buffer would you set, and what signal (a season, a lead time, a promo) would make you change it?
Trains: all 5 pillars, integrated. Field card §1 to §4
