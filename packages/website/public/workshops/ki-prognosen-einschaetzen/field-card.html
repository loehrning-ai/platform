2 The newsvendor rule

Every stocking call weighs two costs:

Cu = cost of being short one unit = lost margin (+ goodwill).
Co = cost of one leftover = unit cost - salvage.

service level = Cu / (Cu + Co)
When stockouts cost more than overstock (Cu > Co), the ratio clears 50%. Deliberately over-order.

Bakery sim: Cu≈4.1, Co≈1.3, CR≈0.76. Croissants spoil overnight, yet a lost sale stings ~3× a leftover. Bake past the average, to ~76% service.

3 Safety stock, in one line

safety stock = z · σ · √(lead time)

Bigger σ (jumpier demand) means bigger cushion. Longer lead time means more can go wrong (grows with the root). z = the service level you demand.

Risk pooling: combine independent demands and σ grows only as √(σ₁²+σ₂²). One shared buffer beats many separate ones (a central warehouse < many local ones; iPhone + AirPods together are steadier than apart).

4 Over- vs under-predict

Let the cost ratio decide, not your gut.

Lean UP (over-order): fat margins · costly stockout · goods keep/resell · slow or unreliable resupply.
Lean DOWN (under-order): thin margins · high spoilage/obsolescence · cheap fast restock · costly storage.

Sim anchors: gadgets CR≈0.84, bakery CR≈0.76. Both over-order. An empty shelf runs ~2× the cost of overstock, which is why the ratio usually points up.

5 Reading a forecast

MAE: are you wrong? Average miss size.
Bias: wrong in one direction? Average signed error. Near-zero bias + big MAE = misses cancel but every order is still off.
RMSE: squares errors; a few big misses dominate.
MAPE: explodes near zero demand, punishes over/under unevenly. No universal "good" number. Beat your own naive baseline (Forecast Value Added).

A 90% band should hold ~90% of real points, and it widens with the horizon. One number pretends the future is certain; the band is where the decision lives.

6 What AI cannot predict

More data sharpens the probable, never the genuinely new:

1 · Novelty: no precedent, nothing to extrapolate.
2 · Regime change: the rules that made the data shift; history expires.
3 · Reflexivity: acting on the forecast changes what's forecast.
4 · Singular choice: a one-off has no distribution to average.

A connected-fitness firm extrapolated lockdown demand, then cut guidance by ~$1B in 70 days and ended with a ~$2.8B yearly loss. Google Flu Trends missed high 100 of 108 weeks. Even weather hits a ~10-day wall: chaos, not compute.

★ The mindset that outlives every model

  1. Start with a baseline. Can't beat "same as last week"? You don't understand it yet.
  2. Let the data pick the model; complexity earns its keep on a backtest, not a slide.
  3. Predict ranges, not points.
  4. Backtest honestly: score on unseen data, across many origins.
  5. Respect the edge: forecast the probable, name the unforecastable out loud.

Benchmark check: on public retail demand data, 5,507 teams competed; only 7.5% beat plain exponential smoothing. The winner (+22.4%) stacked 220 gradient-boosted-tree models; no deep learning. Aggregate demand is predictable; a single item in a single store is mostly noise.
