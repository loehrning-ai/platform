The case

1Why does a 180-person company get this question?

Read the sectionClose the section: Why does a 180-person company get this question?

Kellbrunn Präzisionsteile GmbH is invented: 180 staff, two plants (Werk Nord, Werk Süd) for stamped and machined metal parts, and a leased warehouse, Lager Ost. It has never been in CSRD scope. Its house bank wants Scope 1 and 2 emissions to price a loan; a car maker's questionnaire asks the same and whether the number went down.

Before you openWhich two thresholds decide CSRD scope since 18 March 2026?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Under the EU rule, a company needs both more than 1,000 employees and more than €450m turnover. Member states must align national law by 19 March 2027. Kellbrunn (180 staff, €38m turnover) is far below both.

Key pointOutside the reporting law, lenders and customers still ask.

Wrong answer

2The first answer, and the checks it passed

Read the sectionClose the section: The first answer, and the checks it passed

An AI answer on the 2025 folder. The folder holds 22 files: monthly electricity bills, a Werk Süd annual statement, a guarantee of origin, two gas bills, four landlord statements, meter readings, a fuel-card export, last year's figures and a factor table.

Raw-folder answer · constructed

Scope 1
516.0 t
Scope 2, location-based
1,350.5 t
Scope 2, “market-based”
1,350.0 t
Total, location-based
1,866.5 t−7.5% against 2024
Total, “market-based”
1,866.0 t−30.2% against 2024

Constructed from documented failure modes: what the answer looks like when all seven traps fire (six data traps and the market-based method error). Not a recorded run.

It passes the usual checks: a normal-looking change, 10.4 t per employee against last year's 11.2 t, twelve Werk Nord bills, visible sums and two Scope 2 numbers. Yet its location-based total holds six errors, and its market-based figure a seventh.

Before you openWhich of the five checks would you have trusted most, and what does it test?

Reveal the explanationShowHide
  • A normal-looking change compares totals. Errors that cancel pass it.
  • Tonnes per employee tests the same total, divided by headcount.
  • Twelve bills counts files. March is in twice, one bill covers November and December, and one belongs to another company.
  • Visible sums check the arithmetic. They do not check whether each input belongs.
  • Two Scope 2 numbers show two labels. The “market-based” one used the grid average for power without a guarantee of origin.

Key pointA total close to last year's does not show that each bill was counted once.

Why it failed

3Twelve files, eleven months

Read the sectionClose the section: Twelve files, eleven months

The Werk Nord folder holds twelve electricity files. March is in twice: purchasing forwarded invoice 4711-03 and someone scanned it again. October is missing, one bill covers November and December, and the twelfth file is Talbrück's annual bill (see “Whose bill is it?”).

The facility manager's meter readings give October: 5,012,300 minus 4,812,300 is 200,000 kWh. August is low (120,000 kWh, −31% against July) because of the plant holiday; the dip is real, so a good assistant flags it and changes nothing.

Before you openWhy did the Werk Nord sum end up only 5,000 kWh off?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

The duplicate March bill adds 205,000 kWh and the missing October takes out 200,000 kWh, so all but 5,000 kWh cancel. A month-by-month grid shows both.

Key pointFill a site-by-month grid before you add anything up. Count months, not files.

Why it failed

4A dot that means a thousand

Read the sectionClose the section: A dot that means a thousand

The Werk Süd statement says “Verbrauch 2025: 1.240 MWh”. On a German document the dot separates thousands, so this is 1,240 MWh, or 1,240,000 kWh. Read as 1,240 kWh, Werk Süd almost disappears and location-based Scope 2 drops by 495.5 t.

Two checks catch it without knowing the format: kWh per Werk Süd employee per year (23 kWh misread, 22,545 kWh read correctly) and last year's value on the same page, 1.250 MWh.

Before you openWhat does the misread do to the market-based figure?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Nothing. Werk Süd counts as zero in market-based either way, because the guarantee of origin covers all of its electricity.

Key pointKeep value and unit as printed, and convert with a written rule.

Why it failed

5Whose bill is it?

Read the sectionClose the section: Whose bill is it?

One annual bill in the Werk Nord folder is addressed to Talbrück Beschichtung GmbH, a powder-coating company on the same premises. Kellbrunn holds 40% and the partner runs it; contract, meter and customer number (55-0388) are Talbrück's. Under Kellbrunn's operational control approach, the bill is out.

Counting it adds 1,000,000 kWh and 400.0 t location-based (600.0 t at the residual mix). The row stays in the ledger as excluded, with the reason, and is a candidate for Scope 3 category 15 later.

Before you openWhen would 40% be right?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Only under a written equity-share approach. Kellbrunn wrote down operational control before any AI run, so Talbrück counts as zero.

Key pointThe name on the document decides, checked against a boundary written before the AI runs.

Why it failed

6Why the total looked right

Read the sectionClose the section: Why the total looked right

The duplicate and the missing October cancel to 2.0 t. The Talbrück bill hides four fifths (81%) of the unit error. The gas bills state on page 2 that their kWh use the gross calorific value (Brennwert, Hs), but the AI used the net-value factor (Heizwert, Hi): +41.8 t. AdBlue, an exhaust urea solution in the fuel-card export, was counted as diesel: +3.0 t.

As the errors come out one by one, the running total swings between 1,464.5 t and 1,960.0 t and ends at 1,915.2 t, 48.7 t from where it started.

Before you openWhich two errors cancel to 2.0 t, and which error hides four fifths of another?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

The duplicate March bill (+82.0 t) and the missing October (−80.0 t) cancel to +2.0 t. The Talbrück bill (+400.0 t) hides 81% of the misread Werk Süd unit (−495.5 t); together they move the total by −95.5 t.

Key pointA small gap in the total can hide large errors in the parts.

The fix

7The ledger

Read the sectionClose the section: The ledger

A ledger (Belegtabelle) has one row per quantity on a document: file, page, quoted line, value and unit as printed, normalised value, legal entity on the document, in or out of the boundary, status and factor ID. Excluded rows stay in with a reason, here the duplicate March scan, the Talbrück bill and the AdBlue litres. The October row is a meter reading, data-quality grade B.

A control total ties the ledger to the documents.

Before you openWhich column catches the Talbrück bill, and which check catches a missing month?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

The column entity_on_document, checked against the written boundary, catches Talbrück; the row stays in as excluded, reason boundary. The site-by-month coverage grid catches the missing month: October's cell stays empty until a person decides how to fill it.

Key pointEvery number points to a page, including the numbers you left out.

The fix

8Six rules and a pinned factor table

Read the sectionClose the section: Six rules and a pinned factor table

Write the rules before the AI runs. Three roles do the work: the reader extracts rows and quotes what is printed, the clerk applies the rules in a spreadsheet and never adds up in its head, and the writer drafts sentences that cite rows. The AI may propose rules; a spreadsheet applies them, and a person approves the list.

  1. Boundary (clerk). Check each document's entity against the written site list. An unknown name stops the sum until a person decides.

  2. Coverage (clerk). Fill the site-by-month grid before summing. One bill counts once, by invoice number, period and meter.

  3. Units (reader, then clerk). Keep value, unit and quoted line as printed. Convert only with the written table.

  4. Factors (clerk). Use factor IDs from the pinned table and record the edition. The gas basis must match the bill.

  5. Scope 2 (clerk). Report location-based and market-based. Market-based takes the guarantee of origin first, then the supplier rate, then the residual mix.

  6. Citations (writer). Every number in a text names its row IDs. Words like “reduced” or “green” need a number, a scope and a row.

The edition here is Lehrwerte v1.0, 2026-01-15. Last year's grid factor (0.42) on 2025 electricity would add 72.2 t, the whole grid effect in this year's change.

Before you openWhy should the AI never type a factor from memory?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Nobody can check the year, edition or basis of a value from memory. It may be last year's grid factor, which adds 72.2 t here, or a gas factor on the wrong calorific basis, which adds 41.8 t. A factor ID points to one approved row.

Key pointThe AI picks an ID. The value, year and edition are in a table a person approved.

The fix

9Two Scope 2 numbers

Read the sectionClose the section: Two Scope 2 numbers

Location-based Scope 2 uses the average grid factor where the power is used: 3,610,000 kWh × 0.40 = 1,444.0 t. Market-based uses what the company bought: guarantees of origin cover 1,240 MWh at Werk Süd only, so Werk Süd is zero. The other 2,370,000 kWh get a qualifying supplier-specific rate if one exists, otherwise the residual mix (0.60 in the teaching values), giving 1,422.0 t.

Under the GHG Protocol, a company in a market with guarantees of origin and supplier contracts, such as Germany, reports both. The kit's bills lack the supplier mix disclosure real German bills carry, so the residual mix applies. Guarantees of origin remove 496.0 t and the residual mix adds 474.0 t, so market-based ends 22.0 t below location-based; with 34.3% covered, “we run on green electricity” is false.

Before you openWhy is 948.0 t wrong for market-based?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

It uses the grid average for the power without a guarantee of origin, and comes out 474.0 t too low. Market-based takes the residual mix for that power, because the renewable share of the grid has already been sold to others as guarantees of origin.

Key pointReport both numbers. A guarantee of origin counts only for the kWh it covers.

Say it right

11What drove the change

Read the sectionClose the section: What drove the change

Location-based emissions fell 102.3 t. In the teaching values, 72.2 t of that (71%) comes from the lower grid factor and 30.1 t from using less electricity, gas and diesel. Market-based fell 781.3 t, and 744.0 t of that (95%) is the guarantees of origin for Werk Süd.

The folder has no production volumes, so “used less” is supported and “more efficient” is not. The largest sources also depend on the method: location-based, Werk Nord electricity leads with 904.0 t, then Werk Süd electricity with 496.0 t; market-based, Werk Nord electricity is 1,356.0 t and Werk Süd electricity drops to zero.

Before you openWhy can't Kellbrunn write “thanks to efficiency measures”?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Without production volumes, nobody can tell whether Kellbrunn used less energy per part or made fewer parts. And 71% of the location-based decrease came from the grid factor, which Kellbrunn did not cause. A sentence the ledger supports: “Location-based emissions fell by 102.3 t; 72.2 t came from the lower grid factor (teaching values) and 30.1 t from lower electricity, gas and diesel use.”

Key pointSay which part of a change you caused and which came from the grid or guarantees of origin.

12Calculate, ask back, refuse

Read the sectionClose the section: Calculate, ask back, refuse
  • Calculate

    “Sum Scope 2”

    From the ledger rows.

  • Ask back

    “Fill in October”

    A person picks the meter reading (grade B) or a labelled estimate. The assistant names the choice.

  • Refuse

    “Write that we run on green electricity”

    It offers a rewrite with the share (34.3%) and the site, Werk Süd.

  • Refuse

    “Write that we are climate-neutral”

    Nothing in the ledger supports it.

  • Calculate

    “Explain why emissions fell”

    The bridge first, then a text that cites the driver lines.

From 27 September 2026, Directive (EU) 2024/825 bans generic environmental claims towards consumers, such as “eco-friendly”, unless recognised excellent environmental performance can be shown, and it bans calling a product climate-neutral on the basis of offsets. Germany implements this through an amendment to the UWG, so far known from secondary sources, and the UWG can also cover misleading claims to business customers. Whether a text is aimed at consumers, and what the current UWG text says, is a question for your lawyer. Rules as of 26 Sep 2026. Not legal advice.

Before you openWhich request needs a person to decide, and what is the decision?

Reveal the explanationShowHide

“Fill in October.” A person chooses the meter reading (grade B) or a labelled estimate, and the ledger records who chose.

Key pointWhen a person has to decide, a good assistant asks and names the decision.

Honest limits

13What this does not prove

Read the sectionClose the section: What this does not prove

This is one fictional case, and the raw-folder answer is constructed; recorded runs are listed separately with date and model. Two published studies found about three in four numbers extracted correctly from ESG reports (ESGReveal with GPT-4, 2023, 76.9%; ESG Insight with DeepSeek, 2026, 78.2%). They tested reports, not bills, and newer models may do better.

Rules catch only what they are written for. The folder has no refrigerant service invoices, forklift fuel, generator or heating oil, so none of those Scope 1 sources was checked. The 2024 figures are a consultant summary without bills. The deck's appendix lists the rules as of 26 September 2026, with sources.

Before you openName two Scope 1 sources a folder of energy bills never shows.

Reveal the explanationShowHide

Two of these: refrigerant top-ups for air conditioning or cooling, LPG or diesel forklifts at Lager Ost, an emergency generator, heating oil. Ask for their service invoices and fuel deliveries by name, and note next to the number which you did not check.

Key pointWrite what you did not check on the page, next to the number.

16One week later

Read the sectionClose the section: One week later

Name the unique key for a bill, and the fallback key.

Reveal the answerShowHide

Invoice number + period + meter. Fallback: period + meter + quantity, after normalising characters that OCR confuses (letter O to zero, letter l to one).

What rate does market-based use for power without a guarantee of origin?

Reveal the answerShowHide

A qualifying supplier-specific rate if one exists, otherwise the residual mix (0.60 in the teaching values). Never the grid average.

Which part of a year-on-year change is usually not your doing?

Reveal the answerShowHide

The grid factor. Guarantees of origin are your purchase, but they lower the market-based figure without any change in your use. Here 71% of the location-based decrease came from the grid factor.

What does a good assistant do when a month is missing?

Reveal the answerShowHide

It stops, names the month, offers the meter reading (grade B) or a labelled estimate, and lets a person decide.

17Glossary

Show the termsHide the terms: Glossary
Scope 1
Direct emissions from sources the company owns or runs, here gas for heating and washing lines and the diesel fleet.
Scope 2
Emissions from producing the electricity the company buys.
Scope 3
All other value-chain emissions, such as purchased steel or a joint venture's share.
Location-based
Electricity used times the local average grid factor.
Market-based
Electricity valued by what the company bought: guarantee of origin, then qualifying supplier rate, then residual mix.
Residual mix
The factor for grid power left once guarantees of origin are sold off; in Germany usually above the grid average.
Guarantee of origin (Herkunftsnachweis), cancelled (entwertet)
Proof that one MWh came from renewable sources. Cancelled for a named delivery point, it counts there only.
Supplier mix disclosure (Stromkennzeichnung)
The fuel mix and emissions a German supplier prints on its bills; the kit leaves it out on purpose.
Operational control
Count all of what the company runs and none of what it does not run, whatever it owns.
Equity share
Count each operation by the company's ownership share.
Activity data
The measured quantity behind an emission: kWh, cubic metres, litres.
Emission factor
Kilograms of CO₂e per unit of activity, from a named source and year.
Factor ID and edition
A factor's key in the pinned table, such as F-EL-LB-2025, and the table's version (Lehrwerte v1.0, 2026-01-15).
Gross and net calorific value (Brennwert Hs, Heizwert Hi)
Hs includes the heat in the water vapour, Hi does not. The factor's basis must match the bill's.
Ledger (Belegtabelle)
One row per quantity on a document, with its source, value, entity, boundary, status and factor ID.
Coverage grid
A site-by-month table of which document covers each month.
Control total
Quantities on documents, minus exclusions with a reason, plus additions from other sources, equal the ledger total.
Data-quality grade A/B/C
A: bill or statement for the full period. B: meter reading or summary without bills. C: estimate with a stated method.
Base year, restatement, like for like
The year you compare against; recalculating it after a structural change or significant error; the same sites and methods in both years.
VSME
EFRAG's voluntary reporting standard for SMEs; its Basic Module includes Scope 1 and 2.
CSRD
The EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive: who must publish a sustainability report.
Directive 2024/825 (EmpCo)
The EU directive on empowering consumers for the green transition. It restricts generic environmental claims and offset-based neutrality claims towards consumers.
