# Editorial system

## Four pillars

### Understand

Mental models for language models, training data, inference, retrieval, agents,
multimodal systems, and limits.

### Apply

Bounded tasks in work, learning, administration, and everyday life. Each
example names the permitted data and the human decision that remains.

### Verify

Claim decomposition, primary-source checks, calculations, counterexamples,
test cases, uncertainty, and escalation to domain experts.

### Shape

AI and work, education, rights, environmental effects, open-source choices,
governance, and public contribution.

## Required publication fields

Every canonical article contains:

- named author;
- intended audience and prerequisites;
- one explicit learning outcome;
- publication and last-reviewed dates;
- primary sources;
- legal or technical status date when relevant;
- tested model and tool versions when relevant;
- one practical exercise;
- one verification step;
- a section called "Was das nicht beweist";
- reuse license and attribution;
- correction route;
- links to stronger or deeper external resources;
- disclosure of material AI assistance in research or drafting.

## Evidence classes

1. **Operative primary source:** current law, official guidance, product
   documentation, source code, or original dataset.
2. **Primary research:** the original study with method and sample.
3. **Institutional synthesis:** a reputable explanation that links its basis.
4. **Author observation:** clearly labeled experience, not generalized as data.
5. **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from reviewed evidence and labeled as such.

Never turn a press summary into a legal source. Never combine percentages from
different surveys as if they were one sample.

## Freshness

- Regulatory articles: recheck before publication and at every material legal
  milestone.
- Tool-sensitive articles: record exact version and recheck within 90 days.
- Statistical articles: preserve survey date, sample, population, and source.
- Evergreen mental models: review annually or when the underlying system
  changes materially.

An expired review date does not make content false. It makes its current status
unverified.

## Lifecycle

`idea` → `researched` → `draft` → `reviewed` → `published` → `updated` or
`retired`

Only `published` content receives a canonical website URL. A public repository
draft remains publicly disclosed, even when it is not published on the site.

## Review gate

Reject publication when:

- a central claim has no primary source;
- operative law and a proposal are mixed;
- the copy promises compliance or recognition;
- a product count is hardcoded instead of derived;
- a pending feature is described as live;
- the exercise needs personal or confidential data;
- third-party material lacks reuse rights;
- the correction route or limitation section is missing;
- the derivative channel copy changes the meaning of the canonical source.
