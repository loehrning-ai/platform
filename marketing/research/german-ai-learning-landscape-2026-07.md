# German AI learning landscape, July 2026

Access date for every linked source: 2026-07-26.

## Scope and evidence standard

This review covers free German-language AI-learning offers, adjacent open-education infrastructure, current German and European AI-literacy initiatives, and recent primary survey evidence.

The review uses provider pages, public institutions, official policy pages, and organizations publishing their own study methodology. It does not claim to be an exhaustive census.

Labels used below:

- **Verified fact:** directly supported by a cited source.
- **Editorial inference:** a strategic interpretation of multiple verified facts. It is not a fact about the entire market.

## Strategic conclusion

**Editorial inference:** loehrning.ai should not become another large course catalog. The strongest role is a maintained public knowledge layer between formal courses, fast-moving AI news, and commercial productivity content.

The editorial position should be:

> Open, source-backed, practical AI literacy for German-speaking people who want to understand, use, verify, and shape AI.

This position requires:

- German-first explanations.
- No payment or registration barrier for core learning material.
- Explicit licenses for code, learning resources, and authored editorial work.
- Short role-based paths that link to stronger existing courses instead of duplicating them.
- Germany and EU context for regulation, work, education, privacy, and technological sovereignty.
- Named authors, primary sources, publication dates, review dates, and visible corrections.
- Practical exercises that teach verification and judgment, not only tool operation.

## Comparable free offers

### KI-Campus

**Verified fact:** KI-Campus describes itself as the Stifterverband learning platform for artificial intelligence. It offers free courses, videos, podcasts, and tools for students, professionals, lifelong learners, universities, companies, and public administration. Its original material is generally published under CC BY-SA 4.0. [Source: landscape-ki-campus-faq]

**Verified fact:** its current catalog includes beginner material, generative-AI material, AI Act material, higher-education integration, and role-specific learning. [Source: landscape-ki-campus-home]

**Editorial implication:** use KI-Campus as a destination and potential cooperation surface. Do not rewrite its broad catalog. Add shorter orientation, practical exercises, source maintenance, and role-specific navigation.

### Elements of AI

**Verified fact:** the active German course is free and consists of six chapters covering definitions, problem solving, real-world applications, machine learning, neural networks, and societal effects. [Source: landscape-elements-of-ai]

**Editorial implication:** treat it as a durable conceptual foundation. Add current generative-AI, open-model, verification, and German or EU operational layers.

### openHPI

**Verified fact:** openHPI provides a free German self-study course on generative AI for young people and other interested learners without programming experience. The page states that the course has been available for self-study since 12 July 2023. [Source: landscape-openhpi]

**Editorial implication:** link to openHPI for structured instruction. loehrning.ai can add versioned 2026 explanations and reproducible open-source demonstrations.

### appliedAI Institute for Europe

**Verified fact:** the German AI Essentials course is a free, approximately four-hour self-study introduction for professionals, students, entrepreneurs, freelancers, and other beginners. It covers AI and machine-learning concepts, use cases, benefits, limits, risks, and regulation. [Source: landscape-appliedai]

**Verified fact:** the provider marks the material CC BY 4.0 and offers SCORM, editable Articulate, and MP4 assets for reuse with attribution. [Source: landscape-appliedai]

**Editorial implication:** consider it a high-value remix and referral source. New material should add practical and current value rather than paraphrasing its introduction.

### KI & Me

**Verified fact:** Hochschule Magdeburg-Stendal operates KI & Me as a free platform with self-study courses, videos, podcasts, teaching material, and practical examples related to AI in higher education. [Source: landscape-ki-and-me]

**Editorial implication:** translate suitable higher-education material into general-public and workplace learning paths while preserving source and license requirements.

### KI-Studios

**Verified fact:** KI-Studios, implemented by Fraunhofer IAO and the University of Stuttgart, provides workshops, events, mobile demonstrations, and realistic workplace scenarios. Its stated audience includes workers and employee representatives across sectors, including people without technical knowledge. [Source: landscape-ki-studios]

**Editorial implication:** complement the physical and participatory format with reusable preparation, follow-up, and worker-agency material.

### Mittelstand-Digital

**Verified fact:** Mittelstand-Digital provides free, vendor-neutral support, knowledge, demonstrations, tools, and training for small and medium-sized companies, crafts businesses, and start-ups through a national network. [Source: landscape-mittelstand-network]

**Verified fact:** on 28 April 2026 it described a free 30-to-45-minute self-study offer covering AI capability, limits, critical evaluation, data handling, internal guidance, and qualification measures. [Source: landscape-mittelstand-self-study]

**Editorial implication:** curate and explain these resources without turning loehrning.ai into an SME consulting funnel.

## Adjacent open-education infrastructure

### WirLernenOnline

**Verified fact:** WirLernenOnline describes itself as both a search engine and a community for free educational resources. Its target groups include teachers, learners, parents, and other interested people. It supports contribution, curation, sharing, and interfaces to other educational infrastructure. [Source: landscape-wirlernenonline]

**Editorial implication:** submit suitable school-facing material and cooperate with its discovery layer instead of creating a parallel school repository.

### OERSI

**Verified fact:** OERSI is a search index for open educational resources in higher education, jointly provided by hbz and TIB since 2020. It collects and normalizes metadata from distributed providers and exposes an open API. [Source: landscape-oersi]

**Editorial implication:** publish complete education and license metadata so suitable higher-education resources can be indexed.

## Current policy and public-interest context

### AI literacy under the AI Act

**Verified fact:** Article 4 of the AI Act entered into application on 2 February 2025. The European Commission states that providers and deployers must take measures to ensure sufficient AI literacy among staff and other people operating or using AI systems on their behalf, considering knowledge, experience, education, training, use context, and affected people. [Source: policy-eu-ai-literacy]

**Verified fact:** the Commission says that no single training format, certificate, AI officer, or fixed governance structure is mandated by Article 4. It also maintains a living repository of AI-literacy practices and warns that copying a listed practice does not create a presumption of compliance. [Source: policy-eu-ai-literacy-qa]

**Editorial implication:** publish contextual learning and official-source updates. Do not market a generic certificate as automatic compliance.

### AI literacy for schools

**Verified fact:** the European Commission and OECD released the final AI Literacy Framework for primary and secondary education on 18 June 2026. It contains four dimensions—engage, create, manage, and shape—and 19 competences organized around knowledge, skills, and attitudes. [Source: policy-eu-oecd-framework]

**Editorial implication:** map teacher-facing resources to these dimensions and publish editable, openly licensed lesson assets.

### German implementation

**Verified fact:** the German federal government reported Bundesrat approval of the AI Act implementation bill on 10 July 2026. Its summary assigns the Bundesnetzagentur a central role in market surveillance, coordination, information, and regulatory sandboxes. The source states that the law could then be promulgated. [Source: policy-germany-ai-act-implementation]

**Editorial implication:** date every regulatory article and distinguish operative law, enacted changes, proposals, and explanatory guidance.

## Audience evidence

The surveys below have different samples and methods. Their percentages must not be combined into one dataset.

### General population

**Verified fact:** a representative Bitkom survey published on 26 May 2026 reported that 58% of people in Germany use AI. The same survey reported that 40% feel left behind, 39% feel overwhelmed, and 42% would prefer a world without AI. [Source: evidence-bitkom-population-ai]

**Editorial inference:** an effective public platform must serve skeptical and uncertain people as well as enthusiastic users. Critical explanations are part of access, not an optional ethics section.

### Self-directed learning

**Verified fact:** a representative Digitaltag study published on 24 June 2026 reported that 54% think digital technologies develop faster than they can acquire the corresponding skills. The study reported that 75% experiment independently, 49% use video platforms such as YouTube, and 45% have used AI applications such as ChatGPT to acquire new digital knowledge. [Source: evidence-bitkom-digital-skills]

**Editorial inference:** short self-directed lessons with one outcome, one exercise, and one verification step fit observed learning behavior better than an exclusively long-course format.

### Business adoption and training

**Verified fact:** a Bitkom business survey published on 11 March 2026 reported that 41% of companies with at least 20 employees already use AI and another 48% plan or discuss its use. [Source: evidence-bitkom-business-ai]

**Verified fact:** a separate Forsa survey of 500 companies for TÜV-Verband, published on 28 April 2026, reported that 27% had trained employees in AI, 9% had concrete plans, 17% were assessing demand, and 45% reported no current demand. [Source: evidence-tuev-training]

**Editorial inference:** workplace learning should separate a universal baseline from role-specific material for employees, managers, developers, educators, public administration, and employee representatives.

### Technological sovereignty

**Verified fact:** a Bitkom company survey published on 17 June 2026 reported that 85% consider Germany too dependent on US cloud providers and 80% want major German or European providers. [Source: evidence-bitkom-cloud]

**Editorial inference:** open-source and sovereignty material should move beyond slogans. It should teach open source versus open weights versus free access, model cards, licensing, local inference, hardware limits, and data-boundary decisions.

## Evidence-backed opportunity areas

The following areas are editorial inferences from the reviewed sources, not claims that no other provider addresses them.

1. **Critical beginner literacy**
   - Explain benefits, limits, error modes, manipulation, privacy, labor effects, and environmental costs.
   - Avoid boosterism and fear-based framing.

2. **Short self-directed units**
   - Target a single learning outcome.
   - Include a practical task, a verification step, and a route to deeper study.

3. **Role-based workplace literacy**
   - Separate common foundations from role and risk context.
   - Include employee participation and human decision authority.

4. **Open-source practice**
   - Demonstrate local and hosted alternatives.
   - State license, data, hardware, quality, and maintenance tradeoffs.

5. **A maintained Germany and EU change layer**
   - Show publication date, last review, legal status, source links, and change history.

6. **Verification literacy**
   - Teach claim decomposition, primary-source checks, uncertainty, counterexamples, tests, and expert escalation.

7. **Teacher-ready remixes**
   - Provide audience, duration, prerequisites, learning objectives, editable assets, answer notes, and explicit license metadata.

8. **Navigation before accumulation**
   - Direct learners to the strongest existing resource.
   - Create new material only where it adds original explanation, practice, maintenance, or accessibility.

## Editorial safeguards

- Do not claim that this review proves market-wide absence or exclusivity.
- Do not repeat provider audience or reach claims without a dated primary source and methodology.
- Do not compare percentages from different surveys as if they shared one sample.
- Do not describe proposals as operative law.
- Do not imply accreditation, certification, legal certainty, guaranteed compliance, or guaranteed outcomes.
- Do not publish generic AI summaries without original analysis or practical learning value.
