# Measurement

Marketing succeeds when more people learn, verify, reuse, and improve the
material. Reach is diagnostic, not the mission.

## Outcome hierarchy

### Learning

- starts of the recommended beginner route;
- progress into a second lesson or course;
- completion of a course or exercise;
- return visits to learning material;
- successful use of a verification exercise.

### Public value

- factual corrections received and resolved;
- external contributors;
- documented reuse by a teacher, team, or institution;
- inclusion in an OER index;
- links from trusted educational or public institutions;
- downloads of explicitly reusable material.

### Distribution

- referral sessions by channel;
- canonical article reads;
- article-to-course navigation;
- meaningful comment threads;
- newsletter retention when a newsletter exists.

### Repository

- issues that improve teaching quality;
- time from valid correction to published update;
- repeat contributors;
- published artifacts that pass the admission contract.

## Baseline

Do not invent historical baselines. Record the first complete four-week window
after measurement is explicitly enabled and privacy-reviewed.

## Privacy boundary

- Use aggregate data.
- Do not place names, emails, employer names, free text, or sensitive
  categories in campaign parameters or analytics events.
- Do not activate new tracking because a metric appears in this document.
- Document provider, retention, consent, and legal basis before activation.
- Treat browser-local progress separately from server-observed analytics.

Vercel Analytics and custom events are implementation choices, not assumed
proof. The platform's current runtime and privacy configuration remains
canonical.

## Weekly review

Answer five questions:

1. Which learning job brought people to the platform?
2. Did they reach a useful next step?
3. What was misunderstood or corrected?
4. Which external resource deserved a stronger referral?
5. What should be updated, retired, or taught next?

Do not optimize for impressions at the cost of source quality.
